Quickstart

Go from zero to a private HTTPS server reachable from anywhere by your guest list — and invisible to everyone else on Earth.

1. Create a pen

Sign up at get.hogman.network and claim a name for your network (for example, john-mcafee). Hogman spins up an isolated private DNS zone (john-mcafee.pens.hogman.network) and a keeper page where you control the guest list.

Once your pen is ready, the sign-up page gives you your first single-use join link.

2. Join from your computer

Open your join link on a Mac or Linux computer and run the short command it shows in Terminal:

$ curl -fsSL https://john-mcafee.gate.hogman.network/start | sh

The script asks for your invite link, verifies hogman-join against its published SHA-256 checksum, saves it to ~/hogman-join, and connects your browser to the pen. Your normal internet browsing stays untouched.

Full details (macOS prompts, VPNs, Linux browsers, extra devices, leaving): Joining a pen.

3. Put an HTTPS server inside it

Anything that speaks HTTPS (port 443) works inside a pen. Share a local web server by port — or a directory of static files — with one command:

$ ~/hogman-join share deadmans-switch 3000

✓ https://deadmans-switch.john-mcafee.pens.hogman.network is up.
  Everyone in the pen can open it.

Your server gets a real name and a standard Let's Encrypt padlock in any ordinary browser, with zero open router ports and zero public IP addresses. Add --background to keep it running after reboot.

Full details (always-on servers, LAN targets, dev servers, stopping): Hosting HTTPS servers.

4. Invite your people (or kick them out)

Open your pen's keeper page (https://home.<yourpen>.pens.hogman.network) to mint single-use invite links, appoint admins or a backup owner, suspend someone temporarily, or kick a member off every device in ~2 seconds.

Full details: Running a pen.
