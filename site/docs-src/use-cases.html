Use cases, worked through

What people put in a pen, and how to set each one up. Every example assumes you have a pen and your computer is in it (joining). Apps named here are examples of good free ones, not part of Hogman; any app that runs in a browser works.

A family photo site · A club or friends' forum · A small company's internal tools · A customer portal · Showing work in progress · Your home server, from anywhere

A family photo site, in five minutes

Goal: the family can browse this year's photos from anywhere; nobody else can.

  1. Invite each family member from your home page (one invite each). They open the link on their computer and copy the command.
  2. Put the photos in a folder, then: 
    $ ./hogman-join share photos ~/Pictures/2026 --background
  3. Tell everyone: https://photos.yourpen.hogman.

Going further: for albums, uploads and face search, run a photo app such as Immich or PhotoPrism on a spare computer and share its port (share photos 2283). The app keeps its own accounts; Hogman decides who can reach it at all.

A club or friends' forum

Goal: a private message board for the climbing club, the band, the old school crew, with no ads and nobody reading along.

  1. Run a forum app on an always-on machine (an old laptop, a mini PC, a small cloud server). Flarum, Discourse and phpBB are common choices.
  2. Join that machine as a server: Add a server on your home page, open the link on the machine.
  3. Share the forum's port from the machine, or run Caddy in front of it (Running a server). Name it forum.
  4. Make a few people admins so they can invite and suspend too (Running your pen).

When someone leaves the club, kick them: every device they have loses access at once, and their names are held so nobody can impersonate them.

A small company's internal tools

Goal: a wiki, dashboards and admin tools that employees reach from anywhere, with nothing exposed to the internet.

  1. Raise a pen of your own (raising your own pen), so the company holds its own keys.
  2. Invite each employee. Each one joins their laptop; those already using Tailscale for something else join side by side without disturbing it.
  3. Join the machines that run your tools as servers and name them: wiki, grafana, admin.
  4. Offboarding is one kick: all their devices are cut off in about two seconds.

Internal tools that today sit behind a VPN or an IP allow-list can drop both: they have no public address at all.

A customer portal

Goal: invited customers reach their portal; attackers never even see its login page.

  1. Invite each customer contact. Make invites from the home page (or script them with invite create on your own server).
  2. Run the portal on a server in the pen and name it portal.
  3. When a customer relationship ends, kick that contact.

Hogman isn't audited yet; wait for that before putting anything high-stakes behind it.

Showing work in progress

Goal: let a few people try the site you're building, from your laptop, without deploying it anywhere.

$ npm run dev                          # listening on 5173, say
$ ./hogman-join share demo 5173

Send https://demo.yourpen.hogman to the people in the pen. It has real HTTPS, so service workers, secure cookies and camera access behave as they will in production. Ctrl-C takes it down; run it again later and it's back at the same address.

Your home server, from anywhere

Goal: reach the media server, the NAS or Home Assistant at home from your laptop on the road, with HTTPS, and let the family use it too.

  1. On a computer at home that's in the pen, share the device's local address: 
    $ ./hogman-join share media http://192.168.1.20:8096 --background
  2. From anywhere, members open https://media.yourpen.hogman.

Nothing on your home router is opened or forwarded.
