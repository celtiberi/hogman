What is it, in plain words?

Your own little internet, with a guest list. You make a private network and invite the people you want. Inside it you can have websites, message boards, photo pages, anything you'd normally use in a browser, and they work just like the sites you already visit. The difference: only the people you let in can reach them.

From the outside, there is nothing there. Your sites don't have public addresses, so strangers, scrapers and attackers can't find them, can't load them and can't try to break in. There's no login page for them to guess at.

You stay in charge. You hand out invitations, everyone gets a proper name, and if someone misbehaves you remove them, every device at once, in about two seconds.

What people use it for

Anything you'd put on a website, for people you actually know. You bring the apps (a photo gallery, a forum, a wiki: there are good free ones), run them on a server in your pen, and Hogman makes them private.

A family

Photo albums, a shared calendar, recipes, the family group chat. Only the family can see any of it. No ads, nothing public, nobody else's servers reading along.

Friends, clubs and teams

Your own private Facebook: a message board, events, a members page. The fantasy league, the band, the climbing club, the old school crew.

A business and its customers

A customer portal that only invited customers can even reach, so attackers never see a login page to attack. (Hogman isn't audited yet, so wait for that before putting anything high-stakes behind it.)

A small company

Internal tools, a wiki and dashboards for the team, reachable from anywhere by employees and invisible to everyone else. When someone leaves, they're out in seconds.

A community

A forum, a newsletter, a shared archive for a group that wants its own space, away from bots, trolls and drive-by strangers. Members vouch for new members.

Your own projects

The home server, the media library, the side project you're not ready to show the world. Reach it from your laptop or phone wherever you are, with real HTTPS.

Instead of…

Things you probably use today, and what changes with a pen.

A Facebook or WhatsApp group

Same idea, a place for your people, but it's yours: no ads, no company reading along, no algorithm, and nobody gets in without your invitation. You can have a forum, a chat, photo albums, whatever you like.

A shared Google Drive or Dropbox

Your files live on a server in your pen instead of someone else's cloud. Only invited people can open them, and there's no public link that can leak.

A website with a password

A password page still sits on the open internet, where bots hammer it all day. A site in your pen has no public address at all: strangers can't even find the login page.

A VPN

A VPN hides your browsing. A pen is different: it's a private place with its own websites and its own guest list. Your normal internet keeps working as usual.

Questions people ask

Do I need to be technical? To get a pen and invite people, no: you sign up on a web page, and your guests click a link and paste one command. To host your own websites inside the pen, it helps to know a little about running a server, or to have a friend who does.

Do my people need to install anything? A small program, once. The invitation link walks them through it on Mac, Linux or Windows. A phone app is coming.

Can strangers find my pen? No. Nothing inside it has a public address. From the outside there is nothing there to find.

What if I invite the wrong person? Remove them from your pen's home page. All their devices are cut off at once, in about two seconds.

What does it cost? Free while we test it. Or run it on your own server, free forever: it's open source.

Can you see what we do? No. What your people send each other is encrypted from one device to the other. If we host your pen, we can see who is in it, but not what anyone says. If that's too much trust, run your own.

Everything else

Strangers, scrapers, bots, anything you didn't invite. They can scream at the fence all night. Nothing inside can hear them.

What your swine get inside the fence

Stock browsers. Stock tools. Nothing special to learn.

Proper names

Every pen has its own name, and so does everything in it: alice.yourpen.hogman, blog.alice.yourpen.hogman. First come, first served. Nobody outside can look them up.

The padlock, without the paperwork

Your pen runs its own certificate authority. Sites get HTTPS automatically, and browsers show the padlock. That authority can only vouch for names inside your pen. We checked, by trying to make it vouch for Google. It refused.

Invitation only

You hand out single-use invite links. No link, no entry. How someone earns a link is up to you: a form, a vouch, trial by combat.

Laptops mind their own business

Personal devices accept no visitors at all. Only machines a member sets up as a server can be reached, so nobody's laptop is ever on show by accident.

Walled off from everyone else

Every pen has its own gate, its own names and its own certificate authority, sharing nothing with any other pen. Whoever keeps it never sees what your swine say to each other. Want nobody upstream at all? Raise it on a server of your own.

Sneaking in gets you nowhere

A device that wriggles under the fence without an invite isn't on the list, so it can't reach a single thing. It just stands there, looking guilty.

The boot

Sometimes a clean pig goes bad. Rolls in something. Starts selling crypto. One command and they're back outside the fence, with their sites gone and their names held so nobody can pretend to be them.

Getting in, if you're on the list

One link, one command, about a minute.

  1. You get a link. It carries your invitation and the fingerprint of the pen's certificate, so you know you're at the right gate.
  2. You run one command. It checks that fingerprint, asks what you'd like to be called, and adds the pen's certificate to your computer. That certificate can only ever vouch for the pen.
  3. Bring your other devices. In your pen's home page, press Add a device for a one-time link and QR code. The new device joins as you, under your name.
  4. You're in. Open your ordinary browser and go. Nothing else to install. Already use Tailscale for something else? The tool notices and joins side by side, with its own small connection, so your other network keeps running untouched.

Leave whenever you like

A hosted pen is the same software as a self-hosted one. Ask, and you get your pen's folder: put it on your own server and carry on. Nobody gets locked in.

What hosting your pen lets us do

  • We can't read anything your swine say to each other. It's encrypted from one device to the other.
  • We can see who's in your pen, their devices, and which pen names get looked up. The same as any keeper sees.
  • We could, in principle, add a device to your pen or vouch for its names, because we'd run its gate. Tailscale has the same catch. If that's too much trust, raise your own pen: same software, nobody upstream.
