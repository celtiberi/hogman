Meet the farm

Four things to know. That's the whole idea.

The Top Hog

That's you

The keeper. You run the pen on your own server. You decide who gets in, what they're called, and who gets the boot.

The fence

The network

An encrypted mesh that only exists for those inside it. From the outside there is nothing to see, nothing to knock on and no way in but the gate.

The clean pigs

Your people and their devices

Everyone you invited, each one known by name. They browse, they host sites, they visit each other. It works like the internet, minus the internet.

The horde

Everything else

Strangers, scrapers, bots, anything you didn't invite. They can scream at the fence all night. Nothing inside can hear them.

What your swine get inside the fence

Stock browsers. Stock tools. Nothing special to learn.

Proper names

Every pen has its own name, and so does everything in it: alice.yourpen.hogman, blog.alice.yourpen.hogman. First come, first served. Nobody outside can look them up.

The padlock, without the paperwork

Your pen runs its own certificate authority. Sites get HTTPS automatically, and browsers show the padlock. That authority can only vouch for names inside your pen. We checked, by trying to make it vouch for Google. It refused.

Invitation only

You hand out single-use invite links. No link, no entry. How someone earns a link is up to you: a form, a vouch, trial by combat.

Laptops mind their own business

Personal devices accept no visitors at all. Only machines a member sets up as a server can be reached, so nobody's laptop is ever on show by accident.

Nobody upstream

The whole thing runs on one small server that you control. No company in the middle holding the keys, and the keeper never sees what your swine say to each other.

Sneaking in gets you nowhere

A device that wriggles under the fence without an invite isn't on the list, so it can't reach a single thing. It just stands there, looking guilty.

The boot

Sometimes a clean pig goes bad. Rolls in something. Starts selling crypto. One command and they're back outside the fence, with their sites gone and their names held so nobody can pretend to be them.

Getting in, if you're on the list

One link, one command, about a minute.

  1. You get a link. It carries your invitation and the fingerprint of the pen's certificate, so you know you're at the right gate.
  2. You run one command. It checks that fingerprint, asks what you'd like to be called, and adds the pen's certificate to your computer. That certificate can only ever vouch for the pen.
  3. Bring your other devices. In your pen's home page, press Add a device for a one-time link and QR code. The new device joins as you, under your name.
  4. You're in. Open your ordinary browser and go. Nothing else to install. Already use Tailscale for something else? The tool notices and joins side by side, with its own small connection, so your other network keeps running untouched.
$ ./hogman-join 'https://hs.example.com/join#t=…'

• Checking the network at hs.example.com
  network: bobo.hogman
  its certificate matches the link
  and can only vouch for names ending in .bobo.hogman

• Redeeming the invite
  welcome, alice

✓ You're in. You are alice on bobo.hogman.

Raise your own pen

A small Ubuntu server and a hostname pointing at it. Then:

# install: sets the server up and starts the front door
$ curl -fsSL https://hogman.network/install.sh | sudo bash

# raise a pen and make yourself its keeper
$ hogman-host add mypen hs.example.com
$ hogman-host pen mypen member add you --role owner

# let a clean one in
$ hogman-host pen mypen invite create --note "alice, clean pig"

# show a filthy one out
$ hogman-host pen mypen member kick gary "rolled in something"
removed gary: 2 devices deleted, 0 keys and 1 invites
revoked, 1 names held until 2027-02-17 20:54

Ubuntu 24.04 with 2 GB of memory is plenty, about $12 a month. The installer locks the server down and tells you the rest: manage it over your own Tailscale with SSH closed to the internet, and move the pen's root key somewhere safe.

Run the same curl again to update. One server holds as many pens as you like, each walled off from the others.

Invited to someone else's pen? Nothing to install up front. Open your invite link and copy the one command it gives you.

Don't want a server? We'll keep your pen

Same pen, same rules. You skip the server.

Raise it yourself

Free and open source. One small server, a handful of commands, and nobody but you holds the gate. This is the whole point of Hogman, and it always will be.

Or let us keep it

We run the server; you run the pen. You still decide who gets in, who gets the boot and what everything is called. Every pen gets its own gate, its own certificate authority and its own name book, walled off from every other pen on the farm.

Leave whenever you like

A hosted pen is the same software as a self-hosted one. Ask, and you get your pen's folder: put it on your own server and carry on. Nobody gets locked in.

What hosting your pen lets us do

  • We can't read anything your swine say to each other. It's encrypted from one device to the other.
  • We can see who's in your pen, their devices, and which pen names get looked up. The same as any keeper sees.
  • We could, in principle, add a device to your pen or vouch for its names, because we'd run its gate. Tailscale has the same catch. If that's too much trust, raise your own pen: same software, nobody upstream.

Hosted pens are being set up now. They aren't open to sign-ups yet.

The honest bit

A pen is not a hiding place

Hogman is private, not anonymous. The keeper knows who is in the pen, what devices they have, and which pen names get looked up.

What the keeper can't see

What your swine say to each other. Traffic is encrypted from one device to the other. Your ordinary browsing outside the pen never touches it either.
