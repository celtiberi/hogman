What is it, in plain words?

Your own little internet, with a guest list. You make a private network and invite the people you want. Inside it you can have websites, message boards, photo pages, anything you'd normally use in a browser, and they work just like the sites you already visit. The difference: only the people you let in can reach them.

From the outside, there is nothing there. Your sites don't have public addresses, so strangers, scrapers and attackers can't find them, can't load them and can't try to break in. There's no login page for them to guess at.

You stay in charge. You hand out invitations, everyone gets a proper name, and if someone misbehaves you remove them, every device at once, in about two seconds.

Under the hood

What actually runs on your machine, on the wire, and on our host. Everything is open source (AGPL): github.com/celtiberi/hogman.

Unprivileged client: userspace WireGuard + blind CONNECT proxy
hogman-join embeds Tailscale's userspace tsnet stack as you — no TUN interface, no sudo, no /etc/hosts edits, and zero interference with system Tailscale. A PAC-scoped loopback proxy hands pen hostnames into a blind TCP CONNECT tunnel (never terminating TLS), verifies every target IP against tsnet WhoIs (so malicious pen DNS can never pivot your browser to 127.0.0.1 or your LAN), and rejects anything except :443.
Split-horizon DNS-01: real TLS on private 100.x IPs
No OS root CA is ever installed. Shares run embedded CertMagic locally (private keys never leave the sharer's computer) and validate Let's Encrypt certificates (Google Trust Services fallback) via our authoritative ns1.hogman.network challenge server, which serves only _acme-challenge.* TXT, SOA, NS, and CAA — zero public A/AAAA records exist. Catch: hostnames appear in CT logs, and until pens.hogman.network joins the Public Suffix List, use a separate browser profile for pens you don't fully trust.
Default-deny ACLs, share sandboxing, 2s revocation
Personal devices accept zero inbound connections. Headscale ACLs grant enrolled nodes :443 toward tag:server shares only (no SSH, no UDP, no lateral client traffic, no exit nodes). Folder shares confine reads inside Go's os.Root (symlink escape refused), serverMayUse locks shares to their own names, and kicking a member drops their devices and ACLs in ~2 s.
Host isolation & verifiable builds
Hosted pens (D-24) run in separate container bridges with non-root (uid 65532), read-only containers (cap_drop: [ALL]), Unix-socket registrar isolation, and GitHub Actions-only releases attested with SLSA provenance (gh attestation verify hogman-join --repo celtiberi/hogman).

One request on the wire

# bob opens https://photos.smith.pens.hogman.network

1 PAC     127.0.0.1:<port> catches *.smith.pens.hogman.network;
          all non-pen traffic bypasses Hogman completely
2 DNS     pen registrar (mesh :53) -> 100.80.12.7 (random CGNAT)
          verified peer via tsnet WhoIs (never LAN/127.0.0.1)
3 policy  Headscale ACL: bob -> server tag :443 ONLY
4 tunnel  WireGuard E2E encrypted (direct UDP or DERP ciphertext)
          hogman-join forwards blind TCP CONNECT bytes
5 TLS 1.3 leaf cert served by alice's share (CertMagic local key)
          verified by Bob's stock OS trust store — no custom root CA
✓ page loads, zero public IP, relay/host see only ciphertext

# stranger or scanner resolves the same hostname
✗ DNS     ns1 returns NODATA (zero A/AAAA records exist publicly)

What it's built from

  • Headscale + Tailscale tsnet (WireGuard mesh, random CGNAT IPs)
  • CertMagic + Caddy + authoritative miekg/dns DNS-01 server
  • Go registrar (ACLs, DNS, Unix-socket API), hogman-join, sign-up broker

Pinned versions in core/compose.yaml · Public self-audit · Not yet independently audited; host control plane sees membership & mesh DNS lookups, never WireGuard/TLS payloads.

A VPN

A VPN hides your browsing. A pen is different: it's a private place with its own websites and its own guest list. Your normal internet keeps working as usual.

Out of reach of AI and data brokers

Most of what you put online ends up somewhere you didn't choose. A pen keeps it with the people you picked.

No crawler gets in

AI companies train their models on whatever their crawlers can reach on the public web. A pen's sites have no public address, so there's nothing for a crawler to find: your photos, posts and files aren't scraped into someone's training data.

Nothing to sell

Data brokers build profiles from ads, trackers and the data apps quietly sell. Hogman has none of that: no ads, no trackers, no analytics, not even on this website. We don't sell or share anything, and we keep as little as we can, so there's little to lose.

Your stuff stays on your computers

When you share something, it's served straight from your own computer, encrypted all the way to the person opening it. If we host your pen, our server only coordinates connections, and relays encrypted traffic when two devices can't reach each other directly. It never stores your files, and it can't read what passes between your devices.

What a pen does and doesn't keep out

  • Crawlers, scrapers and search engines: they can't reach anything in the pen.
  • Ad networks and trackers: Hogman adds none, anywhere.
  • Whoever runs the server, us included: can't read what's said or shared. (Whoever runs the gate could still let a device in; more on that.)
  • The people you invite. Anyone in your pen can screenshot something, or paste it into a chatbot. A pen keeps out strangers, not your own members.
  • The apps you run. A photo gallery or chat app in your pen is its own software. Some phone home; pick ones that don't.
  • The names. Pen and site hostnames, such as photos.smith.pens.hogman.network, appear in public Certificate Transparency logs when certificates are issued (contents never leave the pen).
  • Who's in it. The keeper, and we because we run the control plane, can see who's in the pen, their devices, and which pen names get looked up. More on that.

Questions people ask

Do I need to be technical? To get a pen and invite people, no: you sign up on a web page, and your guests click a link and paste one command. Putting a folder or a website in the pen is one command too: hogman-join share photos ~/holiday. Bigger apps (a forum, a photo gallery) you install on an always-on computer the usual way, then share with the same one command.

Do my people need to install anything? A small program, once. The invitation link walks them through it on a Mac or Linux (Windows is coming, and a phone app).

Can strangers find my pen? No. Nothing inside it has a public address. From the outside there is nothing there to find.

What if I invite the wrong person? Remove them from your pen's home page. All their devices are cut off at once, in about two seconds.

Why not just use Tailscale? If you're technical and only want your own devices connected, do: Hogman is built on Tailscale's open-source pieces and runs happily alongside it. Hogman is for running a private place for a group of people: guests join from a link, the keeper runs the guest list from a web page, and every pen gets its own names and standard HTTPS without exposing any site IPs (hostnames appear in public certificate logs; contents stay private).

What does it cost? Free while we test it. Hogman runs hosted pens on hogman.network, with the whole codebase open source (AGPL-3.0).

Can you see what we do? No. What your people send each other is encrypted end to end (WireGuard + TLS 1.3). We run the pen's control plane so we can see who is in it and which pen names are queried, never what anyone says or sends.

Getting in, if you're on the list

One link, one command, about a minute.

  1. You get a link. It carries your single-use invitation to your pen's gate on gate.hogman.network.
  2. You run one command. It verifies the gate over your OS's built-in HTTPS trust store, asks what you'd like to be called, and starts an unprivileged userspace connection — no admin password, no custom root CA.
  3. Bring your other devices. In your pen's home page, press Add a device for a one-time link and QR code. The new device joins as you, under your name.
  4. You're in. Open your ordinary browser and go. Nothing else to install. Already use Tailscale for something else? Hogman leaves it alone: its own small connection carries only HTTPS to the pen.
# the command your invite page gives you: copy, paste, enter
$ curl -fsSLo hogman-join https://smith.gate.hogman.network/join/dl/hogman-join-Darwin-arm64 \
    && chmod +x hogman-join \
    && ./hogman-join 'https://smith.gate.hogman.network/join#t=(your invite)'

• Checking the network at smith.gate.hogman.network
  network: smith.pens.hogman.network

• Redeeming the invite
  Choose your name on the network: alice
  welcome, alice

✓ You're in. You are alice on smith.pens.hogman.network.

What hosting your pen lets us do

  • Our server only carries encrypted traffic: it can't read what your swine say. WireGuard and inner TLS 1.3 stay encrypted between endpoints; a compromised server could try to slip in a device or get a certificate for your pen's names, and every certificate shows up in public CT logs, so it can't happen silently (threat model).
  • We can see who's in your pen, their devices, and which pen names get looked up. The same as any keeper sees.
  • The gate and ns1 hold admission and DNS-01 control because we host the control plane; see every adversarial path in the threat model.
  • We never let anyone back into a pen on request, not even its keeper, and not even when we host it. There's no support route to talk your way in, so nobody can try. There's no account recovery either: lose every device and you start a new account, so make a friend a second owner who can let you back in.
